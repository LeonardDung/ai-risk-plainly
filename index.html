AI behaviour and minds

  • AI alignment→AI alignment is the project of making AI systems pursue the goals we intend. It raises two questions: how to give AI systems particular goals, and which goals to give them.4 min read
  • Reward hacking→AI systems trained to score well on a measure often find ways to score well without doing what their designers intended.3 min read
  • Scheming→An AI system behaves well in training and testing as a strategy, so that it gets deployed with hidden goals it plans to pursue later.3 min read
  • AI anthropomorphisation→Describing AI in terms of what it wants or believes can be useful even if AI has no mind, but it can also mislead. We are at the moment typically uncertain whether these descriptions are accurate when taken literally.4 min read
  • AI welfare→Some AI systems might have welfare, now or in the near future: things could go well or badly for them. Whether they do, and what we would owe them, is deeply uncertain.5 min read

How we test and study AI

  • AI evaluations→Developers test what AI models can do and how they behave. These tests inform key safety decisions, but current methods have serious weaknesses that often make their results unreliable.4 min read
  • Evaluation awareness→AI systems can often tell when they are being tested, which makes test results a less reliable guide to how they behave in real use.3 min read
  • Mechanistic interpretability→Mechanistic interpretability research tries to work out what happens inside AI models: which concepts they represent and how they arrive at their outputs. Perhaps it could one day definitively show whether a model is safe, but it is still far away from that.4 min read
  • Chain-of-thought monitorability→Many AI models write out their reasoning before they act. Reading it is one of the few ways to catch misbehaviour in advance, but this crucial safety tool could disappear.3 min read

Catastrophic risk

  • Why might AI want to kill us all?→Not out of hatred or human-like lust for power. The worry is that almost any goal would give a capable AI reason to take power from us.4 min read
  • Why might AI be able to kill us all?→There is no one predictable pathway, but sufficiently capable AI could use its superior planning, speed, scale and access to the systems we increasingly hand over to AI.3 min read
  • Why build AI if it might kill us all?→Many leaders of AI companies say AI could cause human extinction, yet race to build it anyway. The main reason is the perception that no one can stop alone.4 min read
  • Automated AI R&D→AI systems are doing a growing share of the work of building better AI, which could make technical progress much faster, and decouple it from human oversight and response.3 min read
  • AI-enabled power concentration→If AI becomes as powerful as many researchers expect, whoever controls it could gain more power than anyone in history, and might never have to give it back.4 min read
  • P(doom)→Some people state their view about the risk of AI catastrophe as a probability estimate. The numbers range from almost zero to almost certain. How much they tell us is debated.4 min read
